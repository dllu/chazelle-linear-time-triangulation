# Paper corrections and implementation choices

Section and lemma numbers refer to Bernard Chazelle, *Triangulating a Simple Polygon in Linear Time* (1991). The output stage follows Alain Fournier and Delfin Y. Montuno, *Triangulating Simple Polygons and Equivalent Problems* (1984). The Rust implementation uses the papers’ definitions of visibility, weight and granularity. The accompanying [C++ project’s investigation](https://github.com/Jayyhk/chazelle-triangulation/blob/main/docs/DEVIATIONS.md) identified the publication issues below. [Chazelle](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~chazelle/pubs/polygon-triang.pdf) · [Fournier–Montuno](https://doi.org/10.1145/357337.357341)

## Corrections

**A degree drop can unlock a contraction.** Section 3.3, p. 512, permits testing each chord once in arbitrary order. Consider the chain with successive x coordinates 0 through 8 and y coordinates `[2, 7, -1, 5, 0, 8, 3, 9, -2]`. In its visibility tree the wrap chord from edge 7 to vertex 5 joins two regions of degree at least three. One has degree three and a leaf neighbor. At γ = 16 every merged arc fits. The hub chord fails the initial degree test, then passes after removing the leaf. The implementation requeues the surviving exits after each contraction. At most four exits survive, giving linear queue work. The fixture is `contractions_can_unlock_a_hub_chord`.

**The arc cutter needs an upper bound on the individual granularities.** Full granularity includes maximality under contraction. A one-edge open chain with both endpoint wrap chords is 1-granular. Removing either chord would join the two sides into an arc of weight two. At γ = 2 all chords can be removed. Thus that same two-chord map fails 2-granularity. Section 4.1 reuses pieces with individual granularity at most h, whereas the contract in §3 asks for full h-granularity. Our cutter preserves the individual γ of each aligned piece. The leaf scan in Lemma 3.2 uses the upper bound h on its arc weights. See `maximality_changes_with_granularity`.

**Backtracking in Fournier–Montuno Algorithm 3 must preserve the base endpoints.** On the clockwise polygon `[(0,8), (6,6), (4,4), (1,2), (0,0)]`, the first ear is (0,1,2). The printed predecessor update moves back to vertex 0. Its proposed triangle (4,0,2) contains vertex 3. Our stack visits the non-base chain from one extremum to the other and pops only a strict convex middle vertex. Each vertex is pushed once and popped at most once. This also retains collinear boundary vertices in positive-area output triangles. See `degeneracies_and_paper_counterexample`.

**Root-inside cycles in Lipton–Tarjan.** The cited implementation reports an embedded graph on 13 vertices for which the Step 9 ascent from a triangle vertex reaches the BFS root without meeting the chosen cycle. Its correction follows the tree path through a lowest common ancestor. Our separator uses the layered construction below, which avoids that ascent entirely. The [reproducer and analysis](https://github.com/Jayyhk/chazelle-triangulation/blob/main/docs/DEVIATIONS.md) belong to the C++ project.

## Representation

- Coordinates use the total height order (y, x), equivalent to an orientation-preserving infinitesimal shear. A site records its step along the doubled boundary, its height displacement on that step, and the copy at a duplicated corner. Edge intersections at one height are ordered by orientation of original vertices. All determinant products fit in i128 for the i32 input domain.
- The last canonical chain of a grade can be short. It splits at the last dyadic midpoint and uses previously built operands. This avoids synthetic vertices and changes the number of chains in a grade by at most one.
- At the common endpoint’s height, a fusion exit can hit the terminal site of the arc currently being visited. The walk records that chord and enters the region on the side to which the source edge rises or falls. This handles coincident heights of *chord endpoints* even when all original vertices have different heights.
- Normalization sorts chord records and their sites in O(μ log μ) time for μ regions. The subsequent region traversal follows mate indices. Coarsening computes the new weights with circular links and normalizes once at the end. Both normalization costs fit the merge bound.
- We leave out the closing polygon edge during open-chain construction. Adding that one edge to each vertex’s two finite-hit candidates takes O(n) time. Interior chord endpoints on any one target edge occur in reverse cyclic source order, since interleaving endpoints in the polygon disk would force a crossing. This gives the ordered trapezoid boundaries for Fournier–Montuno in O(n), including when many rays land on one edge.

## Separator for the ray shooter

The §3.4 shooter needs a set of O(μ^(2/3)) regions whose removal leaves components of size O(μ^(2/3)). It uses that set to identify a component containing the first hit, including for rays that wrap at infinity. We construct this division using the three-root-path tree decomposition of a triangulated planar graph. [Dujmović, Eppstein and Wood, Lemma 5.3](https://ics.uci.edu/~eppstein/pubs/DujEppWoo-SIDMA-17.pdf)

1. Project every retained endpoint onto the centerline of the thin polygon. Put a crossbar between the two strands in each interval between successive projections, including the intervals at the chain ends. The boundary, exit chords and crossbars give a spherical embedding of size O(μ). Fan each face to a center. Two regions touching across a polygon edge now have a path via the two crossbar ends. Each auxiliary vertex touches a bounded number of regions. In particular an interior strip face encloses sites of at most one projected point.
2. Let k be within a constant factor of μ^(1/3). Remove the lightest residue class of BFS depths modulo k, costing O(μ/k) vertices. The remaining bands have fewer than k layers. The complementary dual tree of the BFS tree has a bag at each triangle consisting of its three root paths. Intersecting a bag with a band leaves at most 3k vertices.
3. Assign each region center in a band to an incident triangle. Compress the dual tree to these triangles and their branching ancestors. In postorder, cut a bag whenever its remaining subtree contains more than k² assignments and discard that weight. Each cut pays for itself with disjoint assignments. The tree decomposition then separates the band into pieces with at most k² assignments. Across all bands this adds O(kμ/k²) separator vertices. Translate removed vertices to all incident regions. Surviving geometric adjacencies still have their crossbar paths.
4. Preorder sorting, binary lifting and extraction of the band portions of selected bags take O(μ log(μ+1)) time and temporary space. The stored division and the sorted infinity crossings occupy O(μ) space. A query scans the separator and at most one component. With at most four arcs of weight γ per region, its cost is O(γμ^(2/3) + log(μ+1)). This is Lemma 3.6’s bound.

## Up phase

At grade λ, a chain of size m has γ = 2^ceil(λ/5) and O(m/γ + 1) regions. For a subarc of weight w the cutter produces O(log(w+1)) aligned pieces and at most two partial edges. By the separator bound, shooting costs O(w^(11/15) polylog(w+1)). A cutter piece has granularity O(w^(1/5)). The edge-centroid descent of Lemma 3.2 and its final leaf scan therefore find a nonconsecutive-arc witness in O(w^(14/15) polylog(w+1)) time in a bounded-degree region. Piece oracles and decompositions are cached on their first use.

Each fusion walk advances a site or reports a new visible pair, as in Lemma 3.1. Fusing two conformal operands gives bounded degree. Each witness splits two nonconsecutive arcs until the degree is at most four. With w ≤ γ this gives O(m^(74/75) polylog(m+1)) time per grade-λ merge, including normalization. A smaller final operand contributes at most O(m^(4/5)) records. Preprocessing each operand for subsequent queries costs O(m^(4/5) log(m+1)). Summing over O(n/m) chains per grade gives O(n), since the resulting series over dyadic m converges. The same sum bounds allocated space.

## Region-local down phase

Section 4.2 rebuilds a finer submap of each region by merging submaps of a temporary boundary curve with symbolically tilted exit chords. Our implementation keeps the original exit chords as region boundaries and uses a slower reduction in granularity per refinement. It reuses the up-phase arc cutters, ray shooters and conformality search.

For a conformal region of maximum arc weight w > 4 let λ = ceil(log₂ w). Choose the next weight bound s = 2^(λ − ceil(λ/128)). Use the arc cutter on each arc longer than s. Introduce the pieces’ boundary vertex ports, including null chords on the inside of a turn. For each aligned piece longer than s, copy its up-phase submap, reset its granularity to s, and shoot from the surviving exit ports on the region-facing strand. By closure under visibility each shot stays in the region. Every gap on the strand inherits the piece’s weight bound s. The clipped ends each span at most one edge.

These are the fusion chords relevant to the region. The old exits stay fixed throughout, so we can resolve the new mates against the region's arcs and dispense with the temporary boundary edges in §4.2. Apply Lemmas 3.2–3.3 to split nonconsecutive arcs until each region has at most four exits again. These splits only shorten arcs. Recurse independently. A region of weight at most four admits direct enumeration in constant time.

There are O(log(w+1)) aligned pieces per arc. Copying, resetting and normalizing their cached submaps takes O(w^(4/5) polylog(w+1)) time by Lemma 2.3. The reset pieces and the vertices between pieces contribute K = O(w/s + log(w+1)) ports in all. Inserting their visible pairs by searching the current region lists costs O(K²) in addition to shooting. The total degree after insertion is O(K). Each conformality split reduces the sum of positive excesses over degree four, so at most O(K) such splits are needed. A search may test O(K²) ordered pairs and each local shot may visit O(K) arcs. Thus all restoration in this one region costs O(K⁴ w^(14/15) polylog(w+1)). Its exponent is at most 14/15 + 4/128 < 1.

An arc of weight w contains Ω(w) original boundary vertex occurrences for w > 4. Charge the region work to the occurrences strictly inside its longest arc. Siblings have disjoint arc interiors on the doubled boundary. Along a nesting path the integer grade strictly decreases, so each occurrence pays at most a convergent sum of exponentially decreasing costs times polynomials in the grade. Creating the bounded-weight leaves is covered by the O(K) subdivision size at each parent. The initial coarse regions contribute O(n) at most. This yields O(n) down-phase time and space without a whole-polygon sort during refinement.
